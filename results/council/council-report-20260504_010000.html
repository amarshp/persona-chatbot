Council Verdict

Recommendation: Outsider's "define the product" path, executed through First Principles' "retrieval-as-output-fact-checker" lens. Build the verified ground-truth QA set FIRST.

Where the Council Agrees

  1. n=6 is not actionable signal. 4 advisors and all 5 reviewers agree the sample is too small to ship or pivot on.
  2. ST-03 is a stop-the-line event, not a tuning gap. Retrieval injected a wrong answer into a model that would have gotten the answer right — the worst failure mode RAG can produce.
  3. gpt-5.5 has the corpus memorized — that changes what "retrieval lift" even means. All advisors agree on the fact; they draw opposite conclusions.

Where the Council Clashes

Memorization: bug or feature? Expansionist saw it as an unfair-advantage research opportunity. First Principles + Contrarian saw it as making retrieval structurally redundant. Both are right conditional on different products — neither product has been chosen.

More data vs. more definition. Executor wanted Path X (run traps Monday, $2, 2 hours). Outsider wanted user-definition first. Tiebreaker: cheap data on a flawed grader is worse than no data.

Blind Spots the Council Caught (Unanimous)

Every advisor missed: judge-grader reliability is unverified. ST-03 was caught because a human knew the wiki. The LLM judge missed it. The 2→6 V-score, the 2A/2B/2T split, every measurement in this project flows through that same unaudited grader. There is no human-verified canon QA set anywhere in the project. Without one, every score might be measuring "judge agreement with retrieved context" rather than persona quality.

Reviewer 3 added: n=6 is statistically incapable of detecting the effect sizes being argued about. The entire "marginal lift" debate is unfounded.

Reviewer 4 added: fabrication in a fan product is a reputational cost, not just a metric noise. ST-03 severity depends on this.
