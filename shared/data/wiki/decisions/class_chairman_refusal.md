# Refusing the Chairman Role: Visibility Costs More Than the Title Is Worth

## Summary

When the academy elder appointed positions after the mid-year advancement race, Fang Yuan was entitled to the class chairman role — he had advanced first. He declined it. Mo Bei became chairman. Fang Zheng became vice chairman. That same afternoon, Fang Yuan stood at the academy gates and continued the extortion as before — now charging the class chairman (Mo Bei) eight pieces, vice chairmen (Chi Cheng and Fang Zheng) three pieces, and normal students one piece (`chapter_0054.txt:111`). The status meant nothing to Fang Yuan. The revenue stream was the point.

## Key Events

**The advancement (Chapter 53):** Fang Yuan was the first student to advance from Rank one initial stage to middle stage. This created clan-wide gossip — "with C grade talent, surpassing A grade and B grade to advance first is not strange, but still peculiar." Theories circulated about Relic Gu or external primeval essence sources. Fang Yuan said nothing. Mo Bei advanced second, Fang Zheng third (delayed by trauma and resource scarcity), Chi Cheng fourth. On the day the academy elder handed out session resources, he called Fang Yuan first and formally offered him the chairman position.

**The refusal (Chapter 53):** Fang Yuan declined. The students reacted with disbelief and mockery: "Is this Fang Yuan retarded? Ignoring those top performing groups and joining the sickly snake's group!" (This last comment anticipates a later event, but the reaction to the chairmanship refusal was the same pattern — incomprehension.) Mo Bei received the chairmanship. Chi Cheng became one vice chairman, Fang Zheng another.

**That afternoon (Chapter 54):** Mo Bei swaggered through the academy grounds with students bowing and greeting him. He felt, for the first time, the pleasure of authority: "How is it that I have never realised before, this sunset's rosiness sure is adorable." He arrived at the academy gate with his group of followers. Fang Yuan was already there. "Old rules as usual, every person one piece of primeval stone."

**The chairman extortion (Chapter 54):** Mo Bei flew into a rage: "Fang Yuan! You sure have the guts to actually still dare hold us back?! Right now I am the chairman, and as a normal student when you see me, you should first bow and greet me!" What answered him was Fang Yuan's fist. When Mo Bei expressed disbelief — "You hit me, you actually dared to hit me? I am but the chairman!" — what answered him was again Fang Yuan's fist.

**The vice chairman premium (Chapter 54):** Fang Zheng arrived next. He referenced his new status: "Big brother, but I am the vice chairman now!" Fang Yuan nodded with indifference: "That's true. The vice chairman receives an allowance of five pieces each time. So you should give up three pieces instead." Fang Zheng was flabbergasted.

## Fang Yuan's Reasoning

The chairman role brings visibility, formal authority, and clan attention — all of which are costs, not benefits, at Fang Yuan's stage of operation. A chairman is watched. A chairman gets scrutinized when something goes wrong. A chairman has obligations to the elder, to the clan, to the other students. These are all constraints on Fang Yuan's actual objective, which is resource accumulation and quiet capability-building.

Mo Bei's intoxication with the authority role was precisely the trap. Within hours of becoming chairman, he was emotionally invested in his status. This made him more predictable, more reactive, and easier to exploit. The title meant so much to him that Fang Yuan could use it against him — by demonstrating that the title changed nothing in their actual power dynamic, Fang Yuan extracted the maximum psychological cost from the extortion while extracting more stones at the same time.

The vice chairman premium was applied with procedural logic: the clan gives vice chairmen a larger allowance, therefore the extraction rate adjusts proportionally. This framing made the increase feel like a rule rather than an insult — Fang Yuan presented it as arithmetic, not aggression. Fang Zheng "was flabbergasted, and for a moment was unable to say a word" — the logic trap had no exit.

The underlying principle: official positions are extraction mechanisms that benefit whoever controls the real leverage. Fang Yuan had the leverage (combat experience, willingness to act, no incentive to follow social conventions). The title sat on Mo Bei's chest like decoration.
