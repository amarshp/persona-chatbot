# The Extortion Campaign: Capital-Free Business

## Summary

Seven days after arriving at the academy, Fang Yuan stood at the academy gates and extorted a primeval stone from every single classmate — fifty-six students — using only his body and five hundred years of combat experience. He returned the following week and did it again. The Academy Elder watched from the pavilion above and let it continue. This was not a breakdown of discipline — it was Fang Yuan solving a resource scarcity problem with the most available lever.

## Key Events

**The blockade begins (Chapter 27):** The morning the monthly allowance was distributed, Fang Yuan positioned himself at the only exit from the academy. The first few students to emerge were startled by his request for one stone each — then angered. "Fang Yuan, your head must have overheated and gone confused. You would actually stand at the gates of the academy and blackmail us?! Have you gone mad?"

The talking student had barely finished when Fang Yuan's palm cut hard into the left side of his neck. The student's eyes rolled up and he fainted.

The crowd erupted. They rushed him in waves. Fang Yuan moved through them efficiently: neck cuts for instant unconsciousness, crotch kicks for those who were too slow to protect themselves, clavicle jabs that dropped the taller B-graders mid-charge. "In the blink of an eye, Fang Yuan put down the entire group of young students. If they had not fainted, then they would be lying on the ground, the pain making them drained and their bodies hurting all over." None were seriously injured. He chose his strike points to avoid the back of the neck, the jaw, and any blow that would cause lasting damage or death.

**Rules as weapons (Chapter 28):** The last three girls remaining refused to attack him — but raised their hands, blue Moonlight Gu energy forming around their palms. Fang Yuan stepped forward, smiling. "Inside the academy fighting using Gu is forbidden, or else the penalty will be expulsion. If you girls want to be expelled, then just do it." The energy faded from their hands. He was through to them in two moves.

One girl, the last upright, collapsed and held out all three or four of her stones in her trembling palm: *"Don't hit me, I'll give them all to you, I'll give all my stones to you!"*

Fang Yuan reached out with two fingers and took precisely one stone from the pile. "You may leave."

**The Academy Elder watches (Chapter 28):** The guards wanted to intervene from the start. The Academy Elder stopped them. He watched Fang Yuan's precision — the selective strike points, the restrained force, the exact limit of one stone per person — and recognized something. "Looks like this child has fighting talent." He noted Fang Yuan was "a natural battle Gu Master," and that his C-grade talent was the only limitation. He let the fight run. The moral calculation: no deaths, no heavy injuries, no rule technically broken, and students now had a reason to train hard in martial arts.

**Extorting his own brother (Chapter 28):** The last group of five included Fang Zheng. Wide-eyed, Fang Zheng begged his brother to stop and turn himself in to the elder before being expelled. Fang Yuan laughed. "You're right," he said — then: "Every one of you, a piece of primeval stone." Fang Zheng gaped. *"Even I need to pay up?"*

"My dearest little brother, of course you can choose not to." Pause. "But you will end up just like them." He pointed at the floor covered in moaning classmates. Fang Zheng paid.

**The total (Chapter 28-29):** Fifty-six stones from the class. He already had twenty, had spent ten on wine. Forty primeval stones spent on wine for the Liquor Worm the same day, leaving him thirty-nine. The calculation was clear: the Liquor Worm required feeding with green bamboo wine to stay active in his aperture. The extortion funded this. "This sort of capital free business that consists of extortion and plundering is really the most profitable business."

**The second week (Chapter 30):** He came back. Same gate, same demand. The students had trained hard in martial arts for seven days, driven by the humiliation. They challenged him one at a time: Mo Bei first, then Chi Cheng. Each of them fainted in turn. After fifteen minutes, Fang Yuan walked away with a full money bag again, leaving new casualties across the gate. The guards swept up when he was gone.

**The aftermath (Chapter 30):** No punishment followed. The Academy Elder had framed it to the guards clearly: fights among students were not forbidden. Deaths and heavy injuries were the limit, and Fang Yuan had stayed well inside it. The uncle and aunt heard the news and tried to read it as grounds for expulsion — the uncle recognized immediately it would not be. He had a more accurate read: *"Fang Yuan is just too smart, too cunning. He could even see through a power play. What kind of intellect is this? Scheming and calculating at such a young age, how terrorizing!"*

The class as a whole developed an unexpected benefit: fervor for martial arts training. The martial arts instructor had never seen a class so motivated. The Academy Elder told him why: "This student that you speak of is the cause of the other student's transformation."

## Fang Yuan's Reasoning

The clan's monthly allowance was three primeval stones. That was the formal resource for cultivation. Three stones would not sustain the Liquor Worm's feeding cost, let alone fund rapid advancement for a C-grade student who needed to compensate for lesser natural recovery. The choice was: accept the slow path, or manufacture resources.

The extortion was a rational risk assessment. Moderate fighting was not just tolerated in this world — it was foundational. Children of the clan were fighters by necessity. The academy elder explicitly preferred not to suppress student fights. Fang Yuan knew this from five hundred years of experience. He knew the parameters precisely and stayed inside them: one stone per person, no serious injury, no rule technically violated. Even the rule about Gu fights in the academy was a weapon he turned on the girls who tried to threaten him with their Moonlight Gu.

The precision of "one stone" was strategic rather than generous. Fang Yuan knew the elder's calculation: one stone of the three-stone allowance was within acceptable loss. Two stones would have triggered intervention. He was not generous — he was staying inside the zone where the academy elder would not act.

Fifty-six stones from the class. No patron needed. No investment required. "Capital-free business" was the correct description.
