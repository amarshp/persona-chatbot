# The Liquor Worm Strategic Asset: From Revenue Engine to Rank Two Resource

## Summary

The Liquor Worm — acquired in chapters 1–30 from the Flower Wine Monk's cave — continues as Fang Yuan's core financial and cultivation asset through chapters 31–120. It enabled his first advancement to middle stage (explaining his advantage over A and B grade students), he then ran it as a wine business tavern to generate ongoing income, resisted clan pressure to hand it over, and held private knowledge of the optimal fusion recipe for it that the clan's advisors did not know.

## Key Events

**Advancing to middle stage ahead of peers (Chapter 50, 53):** Fang Yuan was the first student to break through to middle stage. The clan's gossip generated theories: "Take the Relic Gu for example... as for theories about Liquor worms and external sources of primeval essence, these ideas were also naturally brought up." Fang Yuan revealed nothing. The Liquor Worm's refining of primeval essence had given him the cultivation speed advantage — he simply didn't announce it.

**The wine tavern income (Chapters 104–113):** After graduation from the academy, Fang Yuan established a wine tavern as a legal source of income. The Liquor Worm was its engine — it refined primeval essence and was offered as a paid cultivation service to Rank one Gu Masters in the village. By chapter 113, the business was described as "quietly earning a fortune." He kept expenses minimal and operated without fanfare, matching his principle of low visibility during capability-building.

**Gu Yue Qing Shu's proposal (Chapter 105):** Qing Shu — the clan leader's ward and a genuinely good-natured figure Fang Yuan knew would die in the wolf tide — made a sophisticated proposal: sell the Liquor Worm to the clan; if the clan successfully refines it into the Seven Fragrances Liquor Worm (a Rank three Gu with primeval essence refining ability), Fang Yuan gets five years of use rights. If it fails, the clan compensates. Qing Shu framed it as Fang Yuan's best path to Rank three utility.

**Why Fang Yuan rejected it (Chapter 105):** Internal analysis: "With his 44% C grade aptitude, advancing to Rank three was almost impossible. In his previous life, Fang Yuan had stalled at Rank two for over a hundred years before finally and coincidentally obtaining a talent-raising Gu worm." Five years of use rights was bait premised on a scenario (reaching Rank three) that Fang Yuan knew was nearly impossible for him. More critically: he had five hundred years of memories including the optimal Liquor Worm fusion recipe. The clan's recipe went Liquor Worm → Moon Harbinger Gu → Seven Fragrances. Fang Yuan knew the superior path: Liquor Worm → Four Flavors Liquor Worm → Seven Fragrances. Both paths preserved the primeval essence refining ability. The clan's advisors did not know the Four Flavors path. Fang Yuan wasn't going to give them the Liquor Worm for them to use an inferior process — or worse, to succeed, leaving him with only a time-limited use right on something he actually owned.

**The monkey king ambush — betting on the Spring Autumn Cicada (Chapter 116):** During a resource-gathering mission to the stone monkey territory, Fang Yuan was cornered by the stone monkey king — a stealth attacker that had been conserving energy waiting for his primeval essence to run out. With 11% primeval essence remaining and no stealth-removal Gu, Fang Yuan deliberately dropped his White Jade defensive shield, stood motionless with eyes near-closed, and waited for the monkey king to attack. At the moment the Spring Autumn Cicada vibrated in his aperture (its warning function), he reactivated the White Jade shield, absorbed the blow, and fired a moonblade the instant the monkey king's invisible form was exposed by the blood trail from the wound. The narrator: "Even without a stealth removal type Gu, nor a wide area attack method, with his rich fighting experience and the will of steel in the face of danger, Fang Yuan could beat the strong in a weaker state."

## Fang Yuan's Reasoning

The Liquor Worm was acquired at the cost of seven nights of bamboo forest searching and green bamboo wine bait. It became one of the most significant assets Fang Yuan held at Rank one. It explained his cultivation speed advantage, generated legally visible income (the tavern), and its fusion path represented private knowledge the clan did not possess.

The rejection of Qing Shu's proposal was a multi-layered calculation: Qing Shu was a genuinely intelligent operator (Fang Yuan respected his analysis), and his proposal was objectively good for most Rank one Gu Masters with Rank three aspirations. But Fang Yuan's situation was specific: he knew he wouldn't reach Rank three on C-grade talent without a talent-raising Gu; he knew the superior fusion path; and he trusted his own management of the asset over the clan's. Giving it up under favorable-sounding terms was still giving it up to people who would use it suboptimally.

The monkey king encounter illustrates the same asset management principle applied in combat: use what you have with perfect timing rather than wishing for what you don't have. No stealth-removal Gu, but a Spring Autumn Cicada warning function and five hundred years of reading combat timing. That was enough.
