# Acquiring the Liquor Worm: Bounded Risk and the Flower Wine Monk's Cave

## Summary

Fang Yuan's first major strategic decision in the second life: abandoning the clan-assigned Moonlight Gu in favor of the Liquor Worm, a Rank one Gu worth far more as a vital Gu for someone with his circumstances. The plan required finding the remains of the Flower Wine Monk — hidden for three hundred years — by using expensive wine as bait, in secret, at night. It was a gamble. He ran it to a hard limit, failed to find anything for seven nights, and on the final night found the worm. The cave also contained something unexpected: the truth about the fourth clan head.

## Key Events

**Identifying the Liquor Worm as the target (Chapter 7):** In the first week of the academy, while classmates listened to the elder's lecture, Fang Yuan slept in the back corner. He was thinking. In his memories, a drunk Gu Master would find the Liquor Worm about a month later by accident, and the Worm would lead him to the Flower Wine Monk's hidden cave. "If I could find that Liquor worm and refine it into my vital Gu, that would be so much better than the clan's Moonlight Gu. In the blink of an eye its already April, there's not much time left." He did not know where the cave was. His memories were imprecise on the location. But he knew the method: wine aroma attracted the Worm.

**Running the search under cover (Chapters 7, 10):** He bought expensive wine — green bamboo wine, the best on Qing Mao Mountain — and wandered outside the village at night, appearing drunk to observers. This gave him cover for the searches. When it rained heavily for three days and three nights and washed away the wine aroma, he was forced to stop. He waited. "A storm may arise from a clear sky, something unexpected may happen anytime. In this world who can do everything without obstacles in his way, having a perfect journey? Fang Yuan's thoughts were very calm; his 500 years of experience had long washed away the impulsiveness that he rarely had in the first place."

**The final gamble (Chapter 13):** After seven nights of searching with nothing, he bought one more wine jar — the last of his primeval stones, essentially. "Right now the night grew thicker, the spring moon shaped like a bow." He identified three advantages converging that night: three days of rain had just cleared the air of turbid energy, leaving it pure for scent dispersal; seven days of searching had eliminated wrong locations; and the green bamboo wine was maximally potent. "With these three big advantages gathering together, if I want to succeed it has to be tonight!"

He moved through the bamboo forest, dripping wine at each spot, waiting, moving. The last drops went onto a patch of wild flowers. He stood with his final thread of hope and felt it begin to dissolve. "It failed after all." He turned to leave.

Then: a gust of wind blew the clouds from the moon. In the sudden moonlight — a bead of snow. The Liquor Worm, clinging to a bamboo pole fifty steps away, its pearl-white body glowing.

**Following the Worm to the cave (Chapter 14):** He retreated two hundred steps so the Worm would not panic and flee. He watched it drink until it was drunk — its tail curling upward with happy rhythm, totally oblivious. Then he followed. He did not rush to catch it; his goal was to let it lead him home. He ran through the bamboo forest after a small glowing ball, through flowers and a mountain stream, up to a waterfall branch and the boulder with the crevice.

Entering meant fighting water pressure and squeezing through a narrowing stone fissure in total darkness. Spear bamboo walls, moss, the sound of the waterfall. He gritted his teeth and continued. Then: red light in the darkness. And eighty square meters of hidden cave.

**The unexpected discovery — the Photo-audio Gu (Chapter 15):** When he disturbed the vines covering the Flower Wine Monk's remains, a voice spoke behind him. Terror. Then realization: it was a projection on the wall, a one-time recording. The image showed the real story of three hundred years ago. The fourth clan head — the village hero — had tried to poison the Flower Wine Monk first. When the Flower Wine Monk defeated him and showed mercy, the clan head used a hidden Moonshadow Gu as a sneak attack. The Flower Wine Monk, enraged by the betrayal, punched him dead. The elders who survived had reversed the story entirely.

**Taking everything for himself (Chapter 16):** Fifteen primeval stones among the remains, a few dead Gu, the Liquor Worm sleeping on the vines. The Photo-audio Gu on the wall was priceless to other clans as evidence — but risky to sell. The other Gu had decayed. "I went through a pretty rough time searching for this treasure, so I should take everything for myself. Why should I share it with others? So what if I've been found out? Without braving the risks, where would you get profit? That Gu master is really cowardly."

He pocketed the stones, picked up the Liquor Worm, kicked away the leg bone that was in his way, and left.

## Fang Yuan's Reasoning

The Liquor Worm over the Moonlight Gu was not a sentimental preference. The Liquor Worm's ability to condense primeval essence — converting initial-stage essence into middle-stage essence at a 2:1 ratio — was a force multiplier that partially compensated for C-grade's slower recovery. It was the best possible Rank one vital Gu for his circumstances. The Moonlight Gu was what the clan gave everyone because it was adequate. Adequate was not what he needed.

The risk of the search was bounded. He set a clear limit: if the three converging conditions (clear air, eliminated wrong locations, best wine) still failed, he would stop and refine the Moonlight Gu instead. He was not gambling compulsively. He was making a time-bounded bet with a defined stop condition. "Fang Yuan liked taking risks, but he was not a gambling addict, and he was not someone who was bent on gambling back what he lost. He had his own limit, he was clear about his own capabilities."

The charm of living — he said this explicitly when he felt the hope dissolving on the ninth night: "This is the helplessness of life, but it's also the charm of living." The very impossibility of certainty, the way effort and luck intertwined — he did not resent it. He found it interesting.

Keeping the treasure rather than reporting it was the clearest possible statement of his relationship to the clan. The clan would have given him a small reward. The Photo-audio Gu could be sold elsewhere. But even the low-value primeval stones and the Liquor Worm — those were *his*. He had earned them seven nights of cold searches, with his own planning. The clan had no claim.
