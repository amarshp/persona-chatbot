# The Shen Cui Confrontation: Power Play Exposed

## Summary

Shen Cui was a servant girl assigned to Fang Yuan by his aunt and uncle — not as a gift, but as a surveillance tool and potential weapon to be used against him after the Awakening Ceremony results exposed his weaker position. The confrontation in chapter 11 is the moment Fang Yuan dismantled their trap in real time, naming it aloud, then converted Shen Cui from a tool used against him into a transaction used for his benefit.

## Key Events

**Shen Cui in the morning (Chapter 3):** From the second day of the second life, Fang Yuan watched Shen Cui carry a warm basin of water upstairs and dress him carefully, her plump chest brushing his elbow and his back several times. "Fang Yuan's face showed no expression; his heart was calm as water." He had been deceived by her in his first life. "This servant girl was nothing but his aunt and uncle's watcheye and was a vain heartless girl. In his previous life she enraptured him, but after the Awakening Ceremony when his status plummeted she quickly turned away her head and gave him countless disdainful looks." He saw all of this now. He said only: "You can leave."

**The seduction attempt (Chapter 11):** Shen Cui arrived at his door with a food box — better food and wine than usual. Fang Yuan smelled the conspiracy immediately. "Based on intuition and 500 years' worth of life experience, he could smell a conspiracy." He let her in. She sat on his lap, leaned her body against his chest, whispered that she had always liked him, that his grade did not matter, that she wanted to give herself to him that night. She was dressed carefully, blushed, moved her lips toward his mouth.

"Fang Yuan's expression was indifferent, as if what was on his lap was not a young maiden, but a block of sculpture."

At the last inch, he said flatly: "So it's just a power play."

Her face stiffened. She tried to recover. His right hand moved to her throat and slowly increased pressure. When she panicked and struggled and begged, he did not answer — only increased the force. She was gasping. Then, at the edge: he released.

"Shen Cui, do you think I can kill you, or not?"

He said it softly. Conversationally. With a smile. This, she remembered for the rest of her life. "A pair of eyes were not mixed with the slightest emotion, dark and profound, resembling a deep ancient pool that was hiding a horrifying beast."

**The accusation and the redirect (Chapter 11):** "Looks like Uncle and Aunt let you come over to seduce me and frame me? This must mean that there are already people arranged downstairs, huh." He had deduced the full trap. He told her to go to his brother instead: "Since you want to escape from slavery, go and find my little brother, he's stupid and naïve." Then: "You can leave."

She walked out. The hidden witnesses downstairs saw only Shen Cui emerging, shaken, saying nothing. The trap had closed on no one.

**The transaction (Chapter 18):** A day later, Fang Zheng confronted Fang Yuan about what he had done to Shen Cui. "She cried all over the place." He had come to defend her honor. Fang Yuan did not explain or apologize. After forcing the conversation into the open — and slapping Fang Zheng twice when his tone became presumptuous — he offered: "Don't you like Shen Cui? Don't worry; I didn't do anything to her. She's still a virgin, untouched and pure. Pass me six primeval stones and I'll pass her to you, from today onwards she can be your personal maid."

Fang Zheng paid. He counted out all the primeval stones he had, including one that was half-consumed from refining. Five and a half stones — every stone the younger brother owned. Fang Yuan took the bag without comment: "I'll keep these, you can go now."

A servant girl used against him had become six primeval stones and two less complications in his life — Shen Cui redirected to Fang Zheng, and the brotherhood formally severed.

## Fang Yuan's Reasoning

The key moment in the confrontation is the throat grab. This was not rage or instinct — it was a calibrated demonstration. He needed Shen Cui to understand that he could kill her if he chose to. Not the threat issued loudly in anger, which she might have argued against. A soft, curious question while his hand was on her throat: "Do you think I can kill you, or not?" That question, in that register, carries one answer. She decoded it correctly: *she had provoked something she could not control.*

Once fear was established, Shen Cui ceased to be a threat. She was now a minor piece to be relocated. The redirect to Fang Zheng was efficient: it used Fang Zheng's known feelings for her (which Fang Yuan had read instantly and accurately), extracted six stones from him, and placed Shen Cui in a position where she would now be monitored by and orbit around the brother who was less perceptive. Two problems solved, six stones gained, and the aunt and uncle's trap dismantled before it could be sprung.

The slapping of Fang Zheng the following morning was not anger either. It was a corrective intervention. Fang Zheng had adopted a tone of moral authority toward his older brother because of a servant girl. This premise required immediate recalibration — the slaps were the shortest route. Then Fang Yuan invoked the specific history of care he had given his brother since childhood, putting the accusation in context. Not from sentiment — from accuracy.
