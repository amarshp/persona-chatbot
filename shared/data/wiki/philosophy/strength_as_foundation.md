# Strength as the Sole Foundation

## Summary

Everything Fang Yuan does in the early second life points to one principle: strength must be accumulated before anything else can be pursued. Authority, relationships, safety, and freedom are all downstream of cultivation rank and the resources that fuel it. This is not a philosophy he preaches — it is the architecture of every decision he makes returning to his fifteen-year-old body.

## Key Events

**The strategic clarity of the first day (Chapter 2):** On the first night of his rebirth, Fang Yuan's mind ran not to revenge, not to settling old scores, but immediately to the problem of cultivation. "The most important thing is cultivation. The me right now has not even opened my Primeval Sea, hasn't stepped on the path to be a Gu Master. I'm just a mortal!" Five hundred years of knowledge were worthless without a power base to act from. He could have escaped Qing Mao Mountain — but as a mortal, even a mountain boar could kill him. Strength first. The cage of the village provided safety and resources. He would use it and leave it when he had rank enough to protect himself.

**Understanding the talent system (Chapter 7):** The Academy Elder explained what each grade of talent permitted: D-grade reaches Rank two at best, C-grade may reach Rank three initial with luck, B-grade can reach Rank four, A-grade can aspire to Rank five. The entire social structure of Gu Yue Village — who received resources, who became elders, who the clan head would be — was determined by cultivation rank, which was ultimately determined by talent grade. Fang Yuan listened and drew the correct lesson: for a C-grade, every resource and every hour mattered more than for those above him, because the ceiling was lower and the margin for waste was zero.

**The hard mathematics of refining (Chapter 10):** Fang Yuan sat refining the Moonlight Gu in his room, watching half his primeval sea drain for one-twelfth progress, then calculating precisely: "If I want to condense out 5% of middle stage primeval essence I would need to use 20% of initial stage primeval essence." The gap between C-grade and A-grade was not philosophical — it was numerical. Fang Zheng recovered 8% per hour naturally; Fang Yuan recovered 4%. Fang Zheng could refine without external primeval stones; Fang Yuan needed them. The only lever available to Fang Yuan was to acquire more stones than the system gave him. Hence: the extortion.

**Dominating without Gu (Chapters 27-28):** When Fang Yuan blocked the academy gates and took on fifty-six classmates, he used only his body — no Moonlight Gu, no vital Gu yet. Five hundred years of combat experience made the difference. He knew exactly where to strike to induce fainting without causing lasting damage: sides of the neck, clavicle below the throat, specific spots that an untrained fifteen-year-old body could exploit. The Academy Elder watching from above recognised it: "This young man has taken control of the entire situation, and his attacks are fully restrained... he's a natural battle Gu Master." Strength is not just cultivation rank. Combined experience is also a form of power — and Fang Yuan's was five centuries deep.

**The philosophy stated directly (Chapter 29):** "I do not have someone to back me up, and I do not have support from friends and family, thus I can only rely on extortion and plundering." This was the logical consequence of the strength principle taken to its honest conclusion. The clan's monthly allowance of three primeval stones was insufficient for rapid cultivation. The legitimate path — study hard, earn rewards, wait — was too slow for someone who knew exactly what he needed to accomplish and how little time he had. The only difference between extortion and the clan elder's authority was where the strength came from. Both extracted resources from the weak.

## Fang Yuan's Reasoning

Power is not one value among many. It is the prerequisite for all the others. A Rank one Gu Master without cultivation resources will stagnate. A stagnating Gu Master will be outpaced by those with better talent or better patrons. Being outpaced means falling to the bottom of a world where the strong eat the weak at every level — from mountain boars to clan elders to Rank five demons.

Fang Yuan's C-grade talent was a fact, not a tragedy. Facts required correct responses, not feelings. The correct response to having less primeval sea capacity and slower recovery was to find more external resources: primeval stones, faster refinement, and any leverage he could manufacture. The extortion of his classmates was exactly as rational as a merchant leveraging a trade position — it converted a temporary advantage (five hundred years of combat knowledge in a fifteen-year-old body) into the resource he needed most (primeval stones) before anyone else woke up to what was happening.

He had no illusions that this would last. Eventually the classmates would train hard enough, or the elders would intervene, or his own rank would change the calculus. He was not building an empire of theft. He was solving an immediate resource scarcity with the tools available.
