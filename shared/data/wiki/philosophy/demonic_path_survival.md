# The Demonic Path: Survival Over Honor

## Summary

Fang Yuan chose the demonic path five hundred years ago and confirmed it in the first moments of his rebirth. The path is not cruelty for its own sake — it is the refusal to let moral performance override self-preservation and ambition. He does not compromise, does not perform regret, and does not frame his actions through society's moral ledger. The demonic path has a clear internal logic that he has lived for five centuries.

## Key Events

**Death and rebirth without regret (Chapter 1):** Surrounded by every faction of justice, bleeding out on the mountain after five hundred years of cultivation, Fang Yuan looked at the sunset and recited a classical verse. He did not plead or panic. "I failed in the end," he thought, "yet there were no regrets." He had known this end was possible when he first chose the path of a demon — "turning into an enemy to the world, still having to face the consequences." With the final thought that if the Spring Autumn Cicada worked he would "still be a demon in my next life," he laughed aloud as the energy engulfed him. The correct posture toward death, in his view, was the same as toward life: clear-eyed.

**Stating the demonic principle directly (Chapter 2):** On the first night reborn, Fang Yuan articulated his road: "Revenge is not my intention, the Demonic path does not compromise." He did not hate his brother or his aunt and uncle, even though they had made his first life difficult and would do so again. Hatred was a form of attachment to others' judgments. The demonic path simply moved forward, removing obstacles when necessary, ignoring them otherwise.

**The Hope Gu legend — the alternative read (Chapter 5):** During the Awakening Ceremony, the academy elder explained the legend of Ren Zu and the three Gu — Strength, Wisdom, and Hope. Ren Zu gave all his years to the first two, and when he was abandoned by them at old age, surrounded by Predicaments, the third Gu asked only for his heart. The moral was meant to encourage the students to have hope. Fang Yuan heard something different: "When Predicaments come, give your heart to Hope." He gave his heart not to the clan's hope, not to the hope of others' approval — but to his own. The Hope Gu inside his aperture was the engine of his path.

**Not reporting the Flower Wine Monk's treasure (Chapter 16):** When Fang Yuan found the hidden cave with the Flower Wine Monk's remains and the incriminating Photo-audio Gu image, his first thought was not loyalty to the clan but a cold calculation. The Gu master in his previous life had reported the finding to prove his loyalty and avoid risk. Fang Yuan found this contemptible: "I went through a pretty rough time searching for this treasure, so I should take everything for myself. Why should I share it with others? So what if I've been found out? Without braving the risks, where would you get profit? That Gu master is really cowardly." The demonic path does not defer to institutions when the math does not support it.

**Framework for unscrupulous action (Chapter 29):** After the first extortion, Fang Yuan reflected on his methods. He was not bloodthirsty — he calibrated every strike to avoid serious injury. He was not erratic — each action had a clear goal. "Every time he acted, he would have a clear goal. Whatever the type of method, whichever would let him reach his goal the fastest, he would use it. In other words, he was unscrupulous in doing things." Unscrupulous is the correct word. Not immoral by his own values — but indifferent to anyone else's.

**The academy elder's own reflection (Chapter 28):** Watching from the pavilion as Fang Yuan extorted fifty-six students, the Academy Elder had his own moment of honesty: "To hold a stick in one hand and a carrot in another – This is the unique way of the upper echelons." The guards who wanted to stop Fang Yuan were themselves slaves to an authority that had been built the same way. The demonic path was more honest than the legitimate path because it did not disguise extraction behind titles and ritual.

## Fang Yuan's Reasoning

The demonic path begins with a refusal: refusal to let external moral frameworks determine what is permissible when those frameworks exist to serve the interests of the powerful. The "righteous path" was not morally superior — it had simply won enough battles to write the definitions. The fourth clan head was a hero in the clan's stories because the clan controlled the story. He had actually cowered and begged and tried to poison a guest.

Survival shameless is superior to dying with honor. Dead men achieve nothing. The Spring Autumn Cicada required his own life as fuel — it was the ultimate bet on survival. He paid it. He got to live again. Every lesser bet thereafter followed the same logic: the method that preserves the vessel of ambition is the correct method.

This is not nihilism. Fang Yuan has clear preferences: he wants to cultivate to the highest rank possible, to understand the deepest secrets of this world, to walk the furthest road. The demonic path serves those preferences. The righteous path would constrain them with rules designed by others, for others.

"Dying for aspiration, those are fools. Surviving shamelessly for their ambitions, those are true brave warriors!"
