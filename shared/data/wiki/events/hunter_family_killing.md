# Killing Without Innocence: The Hunter Family, Chapter 68

## Summary

Fang Yuan took information from an elderly hunter's family by killing them all — before and after obtaining what he wanted. He killed the father (old man Wang, the "number one hunter"), a daughter, and left two young hunters alive only long enough to force them to search the house for a beast skin map. The chapter title is "There is no innocence in nature." This is the clearest statement in chapters 31–120 of Fang Yuan's moral framework in action against targets who were not enemies, not obstacles, and not threats — they were simply people who had something he needed.

## Key Events

**The information extraction (Chapter 68):** Old man Wang's family had accumulated multi-generational hunting knowledge — trap locations, beast territories, terrain maps. "Normally not within a generation but all the way from their forefathers, these experiences and information were obtained at the expense of their blood and lives." Fang Yuan obtained a set of bamboo papers from two younger hunters who had verified the map's accuracy: "There is no problem, lord. The trap areas in these bamboo papers are all accurate."

**The killing (Chapter 68):** Having confirmed the information was accurate, Fang Yuan said: "Mmm, not bad." Then: "But, I don't believe it." Old man Wang jolted. A moonblade flew. "A skull flew into the air, fresh blood spewing all over." The daughter, who had been lying motionless, leapt toward her father's corpse crying out. A moonblade caught her too: "She fell onto the ground, lifeless." Fang Yuan looked at her dead body and said: "At least she looks decent." The two surviving hunters fell to the ground in despair and begged. Fang Yuan ordered them to search the house for the beast skin map.

**The survivors (Chapter 68):** Fang Yuan offered the two young hunters their lives in exchange for finding the map. This was purely tactical — they were more useful alive and finding the map than dead. Whether they were spared afterward is not shown in this chapter; the principle is that the offer was conditional on utility, not mercy.

**Old man Wang's framing (Chapter 68):** Old man Wang had knelt kowtowing expressly throughout the information transfer, shouting: "Lord Gu Master, this concerns me and my daughter's life, I would definitely not lie to you!" The kowtow, the promised truthfulness, the appeal to his daughter's life — none of it created any hesitation in Fang Yuan. He killed the moment the information was confirmed.

## Fang Yuan's Reasoning

The chapter title is his moral framework distilled: "There is no innocence in nature." Old man Wang was not guilty of anything. Neither was the daughter. Neither were the young hunters. None of this was relevant to the calculation. In the food chain Fang Yuan inhabits, the powerful take from the weak when they have reason to. Old man Wang was weak. Fang Yuan had reason. The extraction followed.

The killing of old man Wang immediately after receiving confirmation serves a specific purpose. A witness who has provided information and is left alive is a potential security leak. More practically: a Gu Master killing a commoner hunter family creates no political problem — commoners have no clan, no legal standing, no Gu to retaliate with. The cost is zero. The benefit (eliminated leak, confirmed territory knowledge) is real.

Fang Yuan's comment about the daughter — "at least she looks decent" — is not cruelty for its own sake. It is the absence of any emotional register whatsoever toward the death. He observed the corpse the way he would observe a tool he'd finished using. The sardonic aesthetic remark is the only kind of observation available when no emotional content exists.

This scene is a reference point for understanding the food chain axiom as lived practice, not as philosophy. When Fang Yuan says "no one is innocent" or "self-interest drives all action," this chapter shows what that looks like applied: a family of commoners who did everything right, who kowtowed, who provided accurate information, who appealed to their lives — and died anyway because Fang Yuan had no reason to let them live.
