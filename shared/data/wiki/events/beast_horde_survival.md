# The Beast Horde: Surviving by Letting Teammates Die

## Summary

During a small-scale beast horde triggered by the encroaching wolf tide, Fang Yuan joined Jiao San's team on a night mission. Over the course of the battle, Fang Yuan used the chaos to remove threats — he deliberately cut the knife-scale net that was binding the wild boar king, freeing it, which led to the deaths of his teammates. He then hid inside the dead boar's stomach to survive the wolf pack, allowed a female Gu Master to be killed in the process, then feigned injury to deflect blame. He was interrogated by clan elders for an hour. He gave no openings.

## Key Events

**The beast horde mobilization (Chapter 93):** A small beast horde formed around the village — a prelude to the major wolf tide Fang Yuan knew was coming the following year. The clan mobilized all Gu Masters. Fang Yuan calculated: "If this is the case… Heh heh heh heh." He saw an opportunity within the danger and suppressed his reaction immediately. He joined Chi Shan's group to enter the field, then split off to find his assigned group.

**The wild boar king encounter (Chapter 94-95):** Jiao San's group — including Jiao San, Fang Yuan, a female Gu Master named Hua Xin, and others — encountered the wild boar king. The team encircled it using the red lava python. When enough progress had been made, Kong Jing deployed the knife-scale net — a steel web with blades — to restrain the boar king. Kong Jing called for Fang Yuan to grab the other end.

**Fang Yuan cuts the net (Chapter 95):** Fang Yuan announced he would "help" and fired multiple moonblades into the net, shredding the steel threads. The wild boar king's frantic struggling tore the net apart entirely. It escaped entrapment. "Kong Jing stared with bulging eyes, lost for words." Jiao San's howl: "Fang Yuan, didn't you say you could hold on!" Fang Yuan's response: laughter, then a convincing pantomime of limping and near-collapse.

**Hua Xin dies, Fang Yuan survives inside the boar (Chapter 95):** Lightning wolves swarmed the battlefield. Fang Yuan created an exit by hiding inside the boar's stomach cavity — the wolves could not penetrate it. A female Gu Master from the sickly snake group identified the same shelter and fought him for the position inside. She could not overpower him. She died in the wolves' mouths outside.

**The interrogation (Chapter 97):** Three days after the beast tide, Fang Yuan was interrogated by clan elders for over an hour — the deaths of all his group members except him were suspicious. One elder whose daughter had died as a Gu Master in the battle pressed particularly hard: "Did you intentionally use your teammate — the female Gu Master — as a shield when you hid in the boar's stomach?" Fang Yuan replied: "I don't know. I was very scared at that time and hurriedly tried to hide inside the boar king, but she also entered inside and fought me for the inside position. She couldn't defeat me and was bitten to death by the lightning wolves. I am ashamed." The elder "could not find any handle on him; their hearts were filled with anger but they couldn't do anything." After five repetitions of the same account with consistent details and no contradictions, the elders conceded.

**The breakthrough concealed (Chapter 97):** During the battle, Fang Yuan had broken through to Rank two. He voluntarily disclosed this to the elders — with no stealth-concealment Gu, trying to hide Rank two aura would be futile. He revealed only what he could not hide.

## Fang Yuan's Reasoning

The beast horde was not a crisis. It was an opportunity. The chaos of a night battle obscures intent — who swung what, who retreated when, what exactly hit the net, whether the limp was real. Fang Yuan had planned the deception before he took any action that required it.

The cutting of the knife-scale net was not recklessness. It was deliberate removal of a threat: Jiao San's team had shown in the fighting that they were potentially problematic (the reasons are not spelled out, but Fang Yuan knew Jiao San's motivations were not innocent from the start). The beast horde provided a battlefield with fog-of-war, making the act impossible to prove.

The choice to let Hua Xin die was not cruelty for its own sake — it was priority arithmetic: one shelter position. She could not take it from him. She lost. He survived. The moral weight of the outcome was irrelevant; the math was not.

The interrogation strategy: state the facts that cannot be denied, provide explanations that are strictly true about external events and carefully silent about internal intentions, repeat consistently enough that the story becomes bulletproof through its own coherence. The elder could not find the lie because Fang Yuan told no explicit lies — only omissions, and the omissions left no evidentiary trail.

The Rank two breakthrough: Fang Yuan chose to disclose the advancement because concealing it would have been more suspicious than revealing it. Transparency about the thing he could not hide was itself a diversion — elders focused on the known disclosure and were further from questioning the unknowns.
