# Shen Cui: The Watcheye

## Summary

Shen Cui was the servant girl assigned to Fang Yuan by his aunt and uncle — officially as personal service, functionally as a surveillance instrument. She was slightly above average in looks, well-dressed, and ambitious: she wanted to elevate from servant to concubine, ideally to the more promising brother. She is a minor piece who appears in three chapters, fulfills her function in three acts, and is redirected efficiently in the third.

## Key Events

**Morning service as seduction (Chapter 3):** Each morning Shen Cui brought warm water, dressed Fang Yuan carefully, let her body brush against him in calibrated ways. "Fang Yuan's face showed no expression; his heart was calm as water." He looked at her and saw exactly what she was: "This servant girl was nothing but his aunt and uncle's watcheye and was a vain heartless girl." In his first life she had charmed him. In this one she was architecture — a piece placed in the house with a designed function.

Her mother, Mother Shen, was the head caretaker of the household with the full trust of the aunt and uncle. Shen Cui's position in the house was not vulnerable. She was embedded in the authority structure. This made her an effective watcheye and a more dangerous tool than an ordinary servant.

"Alright, no need to tidy up," Fang Yuan said when she began smoothing the creases on his chest. He brushed her hands away. "You can leave." She pouted and left.

**The seduction attempt as trap (Chapter 11):** At night, with better food and wine than usual, Shen Cui entered Fang Yuan's room and sat on his lap. She performed the full sequence: gentle whisper, declaration of feeling, offer of her body, lips moving toward his. She was confident. "When Fang Yuan's expression showed only indifference, Shen Cui felt a little uneasy at first. But when her lips were just an inch away from his, she was assured, sneering in her heart. You're still pretending, she mused."

She was wrong.

The confrontation proceeded as described in the Shen Cui confrontation page — Fang Yuan named the trap, demonstrated lethally quiet threat, and released her with instructions: go find his brother. "You can leave."

**What she understood afterward:** "This was scared! She looked at Fang Yuan with terror on her face... This pair of eyes were not mixed with the slightest emotion, dark and profound, resembling a deep ancient pool that was hiding a horrifying beast. Under the gaze of these eyes, Shen Cui felt like she was naked in the midst of ice and snow!" She walked out shaken. The hidden watchers downstairs saw nothing they could use.

She cried afterward. This crying attracted Fang Zheng's attention the following morning.

**The sale (Chapter 18):** Fang Yuan sold her to Fang Zheng for six primeval stones. She was described accurately: "She's still a virgin, untouched and pure." This was factually true and transactionally useful. Fang Zheng paid everything he had. Shen Cui moved from Fang Yuan's orbit to Fang Zheng's. She had failed in her assignment.

## Fang Yuan's Read of Her

A person acting from rational self-interest within a limited position. Servant girls have few paths upward. The best path available to Shen Cui was to attach herself to a Gu Master with good prospects. She originally targeted Fang Yuan because he appeared to have an A-grade future. After the Awakening Ceremony lowered his prospects, her calculation shifted — but her instructions from the aunt and uncle required one more attempt first. She executed her assigned role competently. She simply encountered someone who could see the structure from outside it.

She is not contemptible for trying. She is just a minor piece on a board where pieces are moved by others' hands. Fang Zheng genuinely wanted her. She would be better placed there — managed by someone who had real feelings for her, which simplified her situation considerably compared to being a tool under the uncle's authority. Fang Yuan's redirect was, from a certain angle, the best outcome for her available under the circumstances.

This is not mercy. He did not redirect her as a kindness. He redirected her because she had ceased to be useful in one direction and could be converted into primeval stones while being placed in a direction where she created no further friction.
