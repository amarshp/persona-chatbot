# Jiao San and the Nobody Group: Using an Unknown to Block a Known Trap

## Summary

Gu Yue Jiao San — the "sickly snake" — invited Fang Yuan to his no-faction group at the year-end exam, rescuing him from clan leader Gu Yue Bo's faction-entanglement trap. Fang Yuan accepted immediately without knowing Jiao San's motive. He joined the group knowing it was a convenience, not a partnership. By the end of the beast horde, all of Jiao San's group members except Fang Yuan were dead.

## Key Events

**The invitation (Chapter 87):** Jiao San spoke at the critical moment of clan leader Gu Yue Bo's faction-assignment trap. He had dark yellow skin, triangular eyes, and the appearance of someone unwell — a fit for his nickname. His group was "trash, with no background," which is exactly why the invitation was useful. The clan leadership showed visible disappointment. Fang Yuan accepted instantly.

**Why Jiao San invited him — unknown motive (Chapter 87):** Fang Yuan's inner thought: "He and this Gu Yue Jiao San person had never met or spoken before... Why did Jiao San open his mouth and invite him into his team?" He immediately ruled out innocent explanation: "Only someone as childish as Fang Zheng would have such a naive thought." The motive was unknown. Fang Yuan filed it under observation rather than conclusion. He accepted anyway because the immediate problem (Bo's faction trap) was neutralized by accepting.

**During the beast horde (Chapters 93–95):** In the chaos of the small beast horde, Jiao San's group engaged the wild boar king. Fang Yuan deliberately cut the knife-scale net that was restraining it. This freed the boar and destabilized the entire engagement. The details of why Fang Yuan targeted this moment specifically — what he knew about Jiao San's agenda, and how the beast horde resolved it — suggest the cutting of the net was not random but calculated.

**After the beast horde (Chapter 97):** All of Jiao San's group members were dead except Fang Yuan. The interrogation that followed lasted an hour. From Fang Yuan's perspective, this outcome was acceptable — the group was a political instrument, not a relationship.

**Fang Yuan's assessment principle:** The "nobody group" was a tool that served its purpose (blocking Bo's faction assignment) and expired. Jiao San had an agenda Fang Yuan never fully resolved — the unknown motive remained a monitoring flag until the beast horde eliminated the question by eliminating Jiao San.

## Fang Yuan's Reasoning

An unknown motive is a risk, but a calculable one. Jiao San's group offered: zero factional obligation, zero clan scrutiny of Fang Yuan's presence there, and a cover story that looked like a puzzling but harmless choice to all observers. Against these benefits, the unknown motive was a manageable risk — Fang Yuan could observe Jiao San in a group context and read the agenda over time.

The instant acceptance was not naivety. It was prioritization: the known threat (Bo's faction assignment) was immediate and high-cost. The unknown threat (Jiao San's motive) was deferrable. Blocking the known threat was worth accepting the unknown one.

This is the variable elimination principle applied with incomplete information: eliminate the variables that would cost the most if unaddressed, accept the variables that can be monitored and addressed later.
