# Gu Yue Qing Shu: The Good Man Who Will Die

## Summary

Gu Yue Qing Shu is the clan leader Gu Yue Bo's personally raised ward and was the presumptive heir to the clan leader position before Fang Zheng's arrival. He has B grade aptitude at the top of B grade — called "false A grade." He is warm, amicable, loyal, and genuinely good-natured. In the wolf tide one year away, he will stand at the fallen northern gate, use a Rank three Gu beyond his Rank two cultivation, hold the breach, destroy his own aperture, and die as a treeman protecting the village. Fang Yuan knows this.

## Key Events

**Qing Shu's background (Chapter 105):** An orphan like Fang Yuan and Fang Zheng — his parents died in a wolf tide when he was young. Gu Yue Bo raised him personally once B grade was confirmed. His cultivation and character brought him to the threshold of clan leader candidacy. Fang Zheng's arrival with A grade talent ended that prospect. Qing Shu's response: he accepted it and "wholeheartedly took care of Fang Zheng" with genuine warmth. He bore no resentment.

**The Liquor Worm proposal (Chapter 105):** Qing Shu approached Fang Yuan with a sophisticated clan proposal: hand the Liquor Worm to the clan, the clan handles the high-risk fusion, Fang Yuan gets five years of use rights. The analysis embedded in the proposal was accurate — Qing Shu correctly understood that the Liquor Worm's value was in its primeval essence refining ability, that the White Chrysalis fusion route was a waste, and that Fang Yuan's C-grade limited Rank three prospects. He presented the clan's offer as Fang Yuan's rational best move.

**Fang Yuan's private thought about Qing Shu (Chapter 105):** Fang Yuan calculated the rejection of the proposal while also reflecting on Qing Shu himself. The text compares Qing Shu to "Yue Fei, Wei Zheng and Bao Zheng" — historical figures famous for selfless service, moral integrity, and loyalty even at personal cost. Fang Yuan knew Qing Shu would die in the wolf tide "one year later" — at the northern gate, sacrificing his aperture. The knowledge created "a profound impression" in Fang Yuan's assessment.

**Fang Yuan's assessment of such loyalty:** Qing Shu represents what Fang Yuan identifies as the tragic figure of the food chain: the genuinely virtuous person who is also genuinely useful to those in power, and who is therefore consumed by the system he serves with complete honesty. Qing Shu is not stupid — his proposal is intelligent. He is not malicious — his care for Fang Zheng is real. He operates entirely within the moral framework of the righteous path. And that framework will use him as a barricade at the northern gate and call it heroism.

## Fang Yuan's Reasoning

Qing Shu's proposal was turned down not because it was a bad offer — for most Rank one Gu Masters, it was objectively good. It was turned down because Fang Yuan had private knowledge the clan's advisors lacked (the Four Flavors fusion path superior to the clan's Moon Harbinger route) and because he knew his own trajectory with C-grade talent made the five-year use-right premise false.

The deeper note: Fang Yuan recognizes Qing Shu as genuinely virtuous and genuinely doomed by the same virtue. Loyalty to the clan — Qing Shu's chief characteristic — made him the perfect candidate for suicide-level sacrifice when the wolf tide came. The system does not preserve its most loyal members; it deploys them at the highest-cost moment and calls their deaths honorable. Fang Yuan does not mourn this. He observes it as confirmation of the food chain's operation.

This is not the contempt Fang Yuan reserves for cowards or fools. Qing Shu gets something closer to cold respect — the acknowledgment of someone who made real choices and will pay real costs. But respect is not the same as solidarity, and solidarity is not something Fang Yuan extends.
